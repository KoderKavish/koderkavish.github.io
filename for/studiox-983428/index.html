Demo 01: NetworkX for tier-1 mobile operators

Stop the change that should never reach the core.

The public record

At 2:42 AM on February 22, 2024, an AT&T Mobility employee placed a misconfigured network element into production during a routine maintenance window. Three minutes later the nationwide outage began. It blocked more than 92 million voice calls and more than 25,000 calls to 911, and full restoration took at least 12 hours.

The FCC named the causes: the configuration “did not conform to AT&T’s established network element design and installment procedures, which require peer review,” and there were “insufficient safeguards and controls to ensure approval of changes affecting the core network.”

FCC report, Jul 22 2024studiox-ai.com/networkx

Why it opens the demo. Every NOC director has read this report. It turns “autonomous operations” from a risk into the control they wish they had: NetworkX stopping for a human exactly where the policy says to.

First questions in discovery

  • Where does peer review live today: in the change ticket, or in someone’s head?
  • Which changes count as “core”, and who can approve one at 2 AM?
Mission: core change gate

Trigger Change ticket opens in ServiceNow for a new core element, 02:30, maintenance window

  1. 02:30Change specialistReads the ticket and pulls the config diff from the element manager.
  2. 02:31Design-standard agentChecks every parameter against the operator’s element design standard, loaded as knowledge. 1 parameter does not conform.
  3. 02:31PolicyCore-network change, no peer review on record. Blocked. Sent to the Decision Queue.
  4. 02:33Lab-evidence agentNo lab test attached to the ticket. Requests it from the engineer.
  5. 02:48On-call engineerFixes the parameter, attaches the test, approves. You.
  6. 02:49Verification agentWatches device registrations for 15 minutes with the rollback playbook armed. Healthy.

The demo ends on the trace: every check and tool call, logged, ready for the engineer’s manager or a regulator.

Demo 02: FinanceX for finance teams remediating controls

A close the auditor can follow, every day.

The public record

Driven Brands, with over 4,200 locations across 49 U.S. states and Canada, filed its 2025 10-K on May 19, 2026. It restated fiscal 2023, 2024 and three 2025 quarters, and reported material weaknesses in account reconciliations, intercompany and consolidation, leases, and manual journal entries.

Its remediation plan is people: hiring technical accountants, engaging third-party specialists, and building a second-line Sarbanes-Oxley function. It also notes a new ERP whose first stage went live in 2024.

Driven Brands 10-K, filed May 19 2026studiox-ai.com/financex

Why it opens the demo. Three of the four weaknesses map onto what FinanceX already does: reconcile, draft the entry with evidence, and wait for sign-off. The demo shows those controls running every day, next to the people the plan is hiring.

First questions in discovery

Demo 03: ResidentX for single-family rental operators

Deposit statements that hold up.

The public record

In September 2024 the FTC acted against Invitation Homes, which it called “the country’s largest landlord of single-family homes.” It alleged deposits were withheld for normal wear and tear, damage that existed before move-in, and renovations. Per the complaint, from 2020 to 2022 the company returned 39.2% of security deposit dollars, against a national average of 63.9%. The company agreed to pay $48 million without admitting the allegations.

The order bars withholding deposits for normal wear and tear or pre-existing issues. In March 2026 the FTC mailed refund checks to 444,131 consumers.

FTC release, Sep 24 2024FTC release, Mar 11 2026studiox-ai.com/residentx

Why it opens the demo. Every top-five operator’s legal team read this order. ResidentX already has a Deposit agent with itemized charges, photo evidence and human approval. If Invitation Homes is the top-five operator on your ResidentX page, this demo goes to the other four.

First questions in discovery

  • Who decides wear and tear versus damage today, and is it written down?
  • What share of deposit dollars did you return last year?
Mission: move-out deposit

Trigger Move-out inspection is marked complete in Yardi; photos land in SharePoint

  1. Day 0Deposit agentLists the 6 charges from the inspection and pulls the move-in condition report and photos.
  2. Day 0Deposit agentCompares each charge with move-in. 2 items were there at move-in. Removed.
  3. Day 0Policy agentApplies the operator’s written wear-and-tear rule. Carpet after 4 years is wear. Removed.
  4. Day 1Property managerReviews 3 remaining charges with photo evidence and approves. You.
  5. Day 1Documents agentSends the itemized statement for e-signature and updates the Yardi ledger.
  6. Day 1Reporting agentAdds the move-out to the monthly deposit-return rate, tracked against the national average.

The demo ends on the resident’s view: a magic-link page with every charge, its photo, and a dispute button.
