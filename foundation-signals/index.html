More signals for Foundation's sales team

A plan to find more ICP accounts for Foundation, and a lighter track for Distribution.ai. Every company and person below came from a live pull today, so you can see what each signal returns before we build anything.

From Kavish Lodha. Prepared Nov 11, 2026, after our call.

What's running today

RB2B site visitorsEveryone identified on foundationinc.co gets a message.
Ross's post engagersPeople who like or comment on Ross's AEO/SEO posts.
Event attendee listsEveryone at an event Foundation runs or attends gets a message.

All three start from people who are already near Ross or Foundation, then the ICP filter throws most of them out. That's why the leads feel scarce: the pool is Ross's audience, and only a small slice of it works at a $10M+ company that buys AI visibility.

The flip: start from the ICP account list (B2B tech, roughly 50 to 1,000 people, $10M to $200M revenue, North America), then watch those accounts for events that mean "we need AI visibility now". The ICP filter runs first, so nothing gets thrown away.

Six signals, ranked by how fast they'd book meetings

Each one shows its source, why it means the account is buying, a sample I pulled today, and the first line a rep would send.

2. Hiring for SEO, GEO, AEO or content

20+ accounts today
Source
Clay company search: ICP filter plus an open job posted in the last 45 days with SEO, GEO, AEO, content marketing or organic growth in the title.
Why now
They've decided to invest in the channel and have budget. A hire takes 3 to 6 months to ramp; an agency can start next month, or support the hire once they land.
Sample
Sprinto, Secureframe, Katalon, SuperOps, Lifesight, Improvado, Pixis, Telnyx, Poka, MEDFAR, Yodeck, Ambient.ai, Inworld AI, OrangeHRM. I removed agencies and SEO tools from the results.

The job filter matches on title. Before a rep sends anything, the workflow should pull the posting itself and quote one line from it.

"You're hiring a Content Marketing Manager. Here's what that person would walk into: when buyers ask Perplexity for [category] tools, these three competitors come up and you don't."

3. AI crawler rules on the account's own site

Rare, but sharp
Source
A script that reads each ICP account's robots.txt and checks for llms.txt. Free, runs on a Worker in seconds.
Why now
A site that blocks AI crawlers keeps its own pages out of what models learn, so models describe it from other people's pages. Most marketing teams don't know the rule is there.
Sample
Katalon blocks GPTBot, ClaudeBot, CCBot and Applebot-Extended in its robots.txt, under a comment that reads "These bots ingest your content to train foundation models." Katalon is also on the hiring list in signal 2.

1 of 35 ICP domains I scanned today has a block like this, so this signal won't fill a list on its own. It's a finding to lead with when it shows up, especially stacked with another signal.

4. A Profound snapshot as the opener for every account

Uses what Foundation already pays for
Source
Foundation's Profound account. For each account that fires signal 1, 2 or 3, run the 5 to 10 prompts its buyers would ask, and record who gets cited.
Why it works
It turns any signal into a message about the account's own position, which is the thing Foundation sells. The table is useful even if they never reply, so it gets forwarded internally.
Volume
Only the top 20 to 30 accounts a week, so the prompt budget stays small.

5. Engagement beyond Ross

Widens the pool you already use
Source
People at ICP accounts who comment on posts about GEO, AEO or AI search by other voices in the space, by Profound, or by competing agencies. Also people at ICP accounts who post about AI search themselves.
Why it helps
The same intent signal you already trust, but from audiences that don't follow Ross yet. Filtering by ICP account first means only relevant people come through.

6. Public companies telling the SEC that AI search is hitting traffic

Enterprise accounts only
Source
SEC EDGAR full-text search, run weekly, on 10-Q and 10-K filings for phrases like "AI Overviews", "answer engines" and "generative engine optimization".
Sample
Filings since July that match: LegalZoom (10-Q, Nov 4, "AI Overviews"), Klaviyo (10-Q, Nov 4, "answer engines"), QuinStreet (10-Q, Nov 6, large language models and organic search traffic), Intuit (10-K, Sep 9) and Adobe (10-Q, Sep 22, "generative engine optimization").
Why it helps
The company has put the problem in writing to investors, so the opener can quote their own filing to the VP of Growth or SEO lead.

Few results, and many are consumer or very large companies. Worth running because it costs nothing, but it's a side list next to signals 1 to 4.

How it would run

This fits next to the Cloudflare Workers you've already set up. Each signal is its own Worker on a cron trigger, so they can be added or switched off one at a time.

1. ICP baseClay company list, refreshed monthly. Current clients and open deals excluded.
2. Signal WorkersSignals 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6 on daily or weekly crons, written to one table.
3. ScoreICP fit × signal recency, plus extra weight when two signals stack on one account.
4. SnapshotProfound prompts for the top accounts, with the result attached to the row.
5. SendLinkedIn and email sequences for the sales team, and a weekly Slack digest of the top 25.

Distribution.ai is a different motion

Distribution.ai sells a self-serve trial to marketing agencies, content teams, podcasters and creators. Smaller deals and many more accounts, so the plan changes:

  • A partner program is the best fit here, which is your lane. Agencies that repurpose content for clients can resell it or refer it.
  • New shows as a signal: B2B companies that launched a podcast or YouTube channel in the last 60 days have new content to repurpose and no workflow for it yet.
  • Hiring as a signal: agencies and companies posting social media or content roles, the same Clay job filter as signal 2 pointed at a smaller ICP.

A 2-week pilot on Foundation

Week 1: build

  • Workers for signals 1, 2 and 3 on Foundation's ICP list.
  • Scoring, with the top 50 accounts and their contacts loaded into your sequencer.
  • Profound snapshots and written first lines for the top 20.

Week 2: run and measure

  • Foundation's sales team sends; I tune the copy and scoring daily.
  • We track accepted connections, replies and meetings, by signal.
  • Readout at the end: keep, change or cut each signal.

The bar for success: meetings from accounts that never touched Ross's posts, the site or an event. That's the pipeline you can't get today.
